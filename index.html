The task

Proof that survives the upload.

Provenance metadata such as C2PA manifests is stripped by most platforms that re-encode uploaded images. AsymSeal puts the proof into the pixels instead: a camera signs a perceptual hash of the photo it took, and the signature travels as an invisible watermark.

A kilobit

An Ed25519 signature is 512 bits. With the public key (256), a hash syndrome (28) and BCH error correction that fixes any 23 wrong bits, the payload is 1,024 bits.

Invisible

44.46 dB PSNR and 0.9951 SSIM on the test set in the default mode, Balance.

Robust

Real JPEG down to quality 30, downscaling to 0.3×, the two combined, and the Instagram, X, WhatsApp and WeChat settings, at sizes up to 2048 × 1536 and on phone and camera photos of 5 to 13 megapixels.

Cheap to sign

The network always runs on one 512 × 512 canvas, so signing costs the same 0.68 GMACs for a 12-megapixel photo as for a thumbnail.

Decode the copy

Download or share the image first. Until then, a copy is compared with the payload embedded above.

Platforms shrink a photo whose long side is over their limit and compress the smaller image. The copy is decoded at the size it comes back, as in the evaluation's real-platform attacks. Sending in HD, or as a file, keeps more of the watermark, and the 100-bit mode tolerates the most bit errors.

Method

A tiny signer. A strong verifier.

The embedder writes once, on the device, and can never be updated. The verifier runs only when provenance is checked. AsymSeal gives each side the capacity its job needs.

The decoder sets the frontier
+0.79 dB

PSNR at matched bit accuracy from a 32-unit residual decoder, with 34% fewer bit errors at matched PSNR. 64 and 128 units add only +0.06 dB each, not significantly.

Screening study: 51 configurations × 3 seeds, compared at matched PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS.

parameters. No larger embedder we tried improves on this two-convolution write on any readout, including coupling INNs and deep convolutional embedders at 33× its cost.

Training

100,000 steps from scratch on COCO 2017 (256 × 256 crops, batch 16), AdamW at 4·10⁻⁴ with a warmup-stable-decay schedule, on one GTX 1080 Ti in 32.1 hours (the training loop with its inline validation).

Attacks in the loop

From step 5,000, each image gets JPEG at quality 30 to 95, a downscale to 0.3–1.0×, or both, drawn per image: the exact 8-bit operations in the forward pass, a differentiable surrogate in the backward pass.

Six loss terms

L1, NLPD, LPIPS and SSIM structure for quality, bit cross-entropy for the payload, and a low-band term. A loss trade-off drawn per step conditions every block through FiLM; it is fixed at deployment.

Signed payload

A signature in 1,024 bits.

The signer hashes the photo's 512 × 512 view with a 128-bit PDQ-style perceptual hash, signs the hash with Ed25519 and embeds one BCH codeword that also carries the public key. The hash itself is not sent; only its BCH syndrome is.

Step 1

Decode

Read the 1,024 bits and correct up to 23 wrong bits. More errors: decode_fail.

Step 2

Recover the hash

Hash the received image and use the syndrome to correct it back to the signed hash, if it drifted by at most 4 bits.

Step 3

Check the signature

Verify with the trusted public key: verified, otherwise sig_mismatch.

Without a trusted key the key carried in the payload is used, which shows only that the payload is self-consistent: anyone can sign an image with their own key. The signature binds the perceptual hash, not the pixels.

A verifier that already holds the signer's key can leave the key out of the payload and spend its bits on a stronger code, binary BCH(1023, 543), which fixes any 53 wrong bits. Measured at s = 1 on CLIC 2020, 99.96%, 99.9% and 99.8% of signed images then verify after the Instagram, X and WhatsApp settings.

Limits

What it does not do.

Small imagesEvery image is resampled to the 512 × 512 canvas, so a small image is enlarged and the watermark shrunk back to its size. Below about 256 pixels per side even unattacked images lose bits: on one test image at s = 1, bit accuracy 0.878 at 128 × 192 and 0.995 at 256 × 256. Every evaluated size is at least 256 pixels per side.
GeometryNo robustness to geometric attacks: after a crop to 95% of each side, no signed image verified.
Strong downscaling with JPEGIn the default mode, after shrinking to 0.3× and compressing at quality 50, only 4.6% of signed CLIC images verify over the five sizes (21.9% at 2048 × 1536; Resilient: 18.9%); after 0.4× and quality 75, 66.2% (Resilient: 87.5%).
Perceptual bindingSmall local edits can keep the hash within 4 bits: a black box over 5% of each side is accepted for 42.8% of signed CLIC images, an inpainted rectangle of 8% of the area for 12.3%.
Fidelity and the platform goalFidelity (s = 0.80) misses the goal of at least 95% verified under the Instagram, X and WhatsApp settings at every size: 92.0% at its worst size on the test set, 93.8% on CLIC 2020. Balance and Resilient meet it.
AdversariesNot designed against adaptive or white-box attackers who know the model. Not legal evidence; a verified signature shows only possession of the signing key.
One runThe verification rates come from one training seed and one key pair, so no spread is given.
